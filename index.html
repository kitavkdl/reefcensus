      About these four clips

      Asked to count fish. It also found the animals nobody asked for.

      Give it baited-camera video and it gives back MaxN for each animal, when each first arrived, and the frame behind every count. Anything it is not sure of waits in a short queue for a person to answer (Yes, Rename, Not an animal). The table downloads as CSV, also in GlobalArchive layout. Below are four 12-second deep-sea clips with every answer already computed, so nothing runs here. Click a clip.

      How: Muse Spark looks first and names what is there; SAM 3.1, which only sees what it is told to look for, then tracks each named animal frame by frame, and Spark checks its work.

      A count like 4 to 6 is not settled yet: 4 counts only the tracks it was sure of, 6 if every track waiting in the queue is real. Answering the queue closes the range.

      Tested on 24 luderick seagrass clips of 1 to 12 s (remote underwater video, not baited camera) not used in development: MaxN was within one fish of two separate hand counts on all 24 (the author, not a marine biologist, and a second person who never saw the output), and closer to them than the dataset’s reference boxes.
      DetailsSummed MaxN error against the hand counts: 6 against the reference boxes’ 16 for the first count (21 of 24 counted blind) and 2 against 14 for the second person (exact on 22 of 24). The reference boxes were drawn to train a luderick detector, not to count MaxN. Of 40 randomly drawn boxes the reference called false none was judged not a fish (29 fish, 2 part of a fish, 9 unsure). Reviewing the pipeline’s extra tracks on a phone, one person added 145 missing fish boxes to the reference in 9 min 39 s.

      Every model answer is precomputed so there is nothing to install; answer the review queue right here. Run your own clips & read the paper: GitHub · 3-minute video
